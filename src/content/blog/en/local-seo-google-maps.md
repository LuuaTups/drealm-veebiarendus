When someone searches for “dentist city centre” or “plumber Tartu”, Google often shows a map with three businesses at the top. For many local companies that is where most new clients come from. The good news: you don’t have to be the biggest or the oldest to get there, just the best described and most trustworthy.

## What drives local rankings

Google weighs three things in local search:

- **relevance**: how well your profile and website match the search;
- **distance**: how close you are to the searcher or searched area;
- **prominence**: reviews, mentions and overall activity.

You can’t change distance, but you can change relevance and prominence. That is what these steps are about.

## 1. Complete your Business Profile

With an incomplete profile, Google often prefers a competitor. Make sure you have:

- the right **primary category** (e.g. “Dental clinic”, not just “Health”) plus a few secondary ones;
- **services** with short descriptions;
- **opening hours**, including holidays;
- **real photos**: premises, team, completed work;
- a **website link** and phone number that match your website.

Post occasionally: a new service, an offer or recent work. It shows the business is active.

## 2. Collect reviews systematically

Reviews are one of the strongest local signals and they also decide whether someone calls you. Most happy clients will write one if asked and it’s easy.

- After a job, send a short message with a direct link to the review page.
- Reply to every review, including negative ones, calmly and specifically.
- Don’t buy reviews or trade them for discounts: it breaks Google’s rules.

## 3. Make your website strong for local search

The map and your website support each other. Your website should have:

- separate **service pages** for each main service;
- **area pages** only for towns or districts where you really operate, with real content, not one template;
- your business **name, address and phone** identical everywhere;
- a fast **mobile view**, because most local searches happen on phones.

## 4. Measure what brings calls

Your Business Profile shows how many calls, direction requests and website visits it brought. Check these once a month. If calls grow, you’re on the right track. If not, revisit categories, photos and reviews.

## In short

Local SEO isn’t a quick trick but solid groundwork: a complete profile, steady reviews and a website that clearly says what you offer and where.
