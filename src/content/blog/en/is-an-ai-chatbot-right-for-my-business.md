AI chatbots have become so good that many business owners ask: should we have one on our website too? The answer depends on what your customers ask and how much time answering takes today. Below we break down when a chatbot pays off and when it’s just one more thing.

## When an AI chatbot pays off

- **Customers keep asking the same things.** Prices, terms, delivery times, opening hours, sizes, how to book. If the same answer gets written dozens of times a week, AI can take over.
- **Questions come outside office hours.** A customer who gets an answer right away buys or books more often than one who waits until morning.
- **The information exists but is hard to find.** If answers are scattered across pages or PDFs, AI finds them faster than the customer.
- **You want to pre-qualify enquiries.** A chatbot can ask a few clarifying questions and hand your team a proper enquiry.

## When it doesn’t pay off

- **There are few questions.** If you get a couple of enquiries a week, you’ll answer them faster yourself than setting up a chatbot.
- **Every case is unique.** If the answer always depends on a long conversation and assessment, a good contact form and a quick human reply work better.
- **Information keeps changing and nobody updates it.** AI only answers as well as the information it’s given.

## What a good AI chatbot can do

- answer from your website, price list and terms;
- speak Estonian, English and Russian;
- say honestly when it doesn’t know and hand the question to a person;
- collect contact details when a customer wants a quote;
- for online stores, give order status and recommend suitable products.

## How to avoid wrong answers

The biggest fear is that AI tells a customer something wrong. Three things help:

1. **Answers only from your information.** The chatbot must not answer from general knowledge, only from the material you provide.
2. **Clear handover to a person.** If a question is complex or information is missing, the bot says so and collects a contact.
3. **Regular review.** Read the conversations in the first weeks and add information where the bot struggled.

## Where to start

1. **Write down the 20 most common questions** and their answers. That often covers most enquiries.
2. **Start with one channel,** usually your website.
3. **Measure:** how many conversations, how many were handed to a person and how many enquiries came in.
4. **Expand** if results are good: Messenger, email or an internal assistant for staff.

## Summary

An AI chatbot pays off when customers keep asking the same things, also outside office hours, and the information exists. A good bot answers only from your information, says honestly when it doesn’t know and hands complex questions to a person.
