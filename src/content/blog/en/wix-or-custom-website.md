“Couldn’t I just build a site myself with Wix?” is one of the most common questions small business owners ask before ordering a website. Honestly: sometimes you can. Site builders like Wix, Squarespace and Webflow are good tools. The question is what the website needs to do for your business.

## When Wix or another builder is enough

- **You’re just starting** and want to test a business idea quickly.
- **The site is a business card:** contacts, a few sentences about the service, some photos.
- **New customers come from elsewhere,** such as referrals, social media or an existing customer base, not from Google.
- **You have time** to build, update and maintain it yourself.

In that case, a monthly fee of a few tens of euros plus your own time is a sensible investment. Ordering a custom website would be premature.

## When a custom website pays off

- **New customers look for you on Google.** Then speed, structure and technical SEO matter. Builders limit these and are often slower.
- **The site has to sell.** A good website guides visitors along a clear path to an enquiry. A template is made for “everyone” and doesn’t know your customer’s journey.
- **You need something specific:** a price calculator, booking, an apartment selector, a client portal or an integration with another system.
- **Your brand needs to stand out.** If competitors use the same template, your site looks like theirs.
- **Your time is expensive.** If an hour of your work is worth more than an hour of a developer’s, do what you do best.

## What to compare

**Total cost over three years.** A builder’s monthly fee over three years plus your own time can add up to the price of a custom site. A custom site costs more upfront and little per month, especially with affordable hosting.

**Ownership.** A builder site usually can’t be moved elsewhere. If you switch later, you start from zero. With a custom site, code, content and domain should be yours. Always confirm this before signing.

**Speed and SEO.** Test the site on a phone with a slow connection. If it takes several seconds to load, some visitors leave before seeing anything. Google also considers speed in rankings.

**Who maintains it.** A builder handles the tech, but updating content is your job. With a custom site, agree who makes changes and what they cost.

## A third option: start simple and grow

Not everything has to be ready on day one. It often makes sense to start with a small but well-built site: homepage, service pages and contact. As the business grows, add a blog, industry pages, a calculator or booking. You don’t pay upfront for things you don’t need yet, and you don’t have to rebuild later.

## Summary

Wix works when the site is a business card and customers come from elsewhere. A custom website pays off when customers search on Google, the site has to sell, you need custom features or want your brand to stand out. Compare total cost over three years, ownership, speed and maintenance.
