Ask anyone who builds websites what one costs and the honest answer starts with “it depends”. That isn’t a dodge. The word “website” covers everything from a three-page business card to a multilingual portal with hundreds of products. Below we break down the typical price ranges on the Estonian market, what actually drives the cost, and what you’ll keep paying after launch.

Every figure here is approximate and reflects typical market ranges rather than a fixed price list. The real price of a specific project only becomes clear once you know exactly what’s needed.

## Four routes and what they typically cost

**1. Do it yourself (Wix, Squarespace, Webflow and similar)**
Expect roughly €10–40 a month, plus your own time. For a simple site, especially while you’re still testing a business idea, that’s a perfectly sensible choice. The hidden cost lies elsewhere: designs often look templated, speed and technical SEO are limited, and moving off the platform later is painful.

**2. A freelancer**
A simple site usually costs somewhere around €300–1,500. A good freelancer can be a great option, but quality varies widely. Ask who will maintain the site afterwards, who owns the domain and logins, and what happens if that person is no longer available.

**3. A small studio**
A website for a small or medium-sized business often lands in the €900–4,000 range. You get a considered structure, custom design, technical SEO and a clear process, without the overheads of a large agency.

**4. An agency**
Larger projects with several stakeholders, brand work, complex integrations and dedicated project management often start around €4,000 and can go well beyond €20,000. What you pay for is a team and a process built for projects of that size.

Which route is right depends mostly on what the site has to do for your business. If it’s simply somewhere to list your contact details, paying agency rates makes no sense. But if a large share of new customers find you through Google, look at the total cost over three years: a cheap site that has to be rebuilt after a year, or that never generates enquiries, ends up being the most expensive option.

## What really drives the price

It’s less about the number of pages and more about how much thinking and customisation each page needs. The main factors:

- **Structure and content.** Do you already have copy and photos, or do they need to be created? Missing content is one of the most common reasons projects drag on.
- **Design.** A template is cheaper; a custom design costs more but sets you apart from competitors.
- **Languages.** Each extra language means translation, separate URLs and metadata, and more testing.
- **Functionality.** Booking, a shop, customer accounts, calculators or integrations with accounting and CRM tools all add work.
- **Content management.** Want to edit content yourself? The CMS has to be set up and the content model thought through.
- **Technical SEO and speed.** A sound heading structure, metadata, structured data, a sitemap and fast loading don’t happen by accident.

## Running costs people forget

A website isn’t a one-off expense. Budget for the following (all approximate):

- **Domain:** an .ee domain costs roughly €10–25 a year, depending on the registrar.
- **Hosting:** hosting a static site (on Vercel, for example) can be free at low traffic or cost up to a few tens of euros a month; WordPress hosting often runs €5–30 a month.
- **Maintenance:** updating WordPress and its plugins, backups and security often cost €30–150 a month. A static site needs far less of this.
- **Content updates and new features:** new pages, campaigns and changes, billed hourly or as a package.

## Red flags in a quote

A cheap quote isn’t automatically bad and an expensive one isn’t automatically good. Still, a few things should make you pause:

- **The domain or accounts are registered in the developer’s name.** Your domain should belong to your company.
- **The quote doesn’t describe the work.** If all it says is “website, €800”, you have no way of knowing what you’re getting.
- **Technical SEO isn’t mentioned at all.** A beautiful site that nobody finds won’t bring in customers.
- **Someone promises a guaranteed spot on Google’s first page.** Nobody can honestly guarantee that.
- **Maintenance is mandatory and long-term** without any clarity on what it actually covers.
- **No previous work is shown,** or there’s no way to check it.

## How to get an accurate price

The fastest way to a realistic number is to answer a few questions up front: what is the main goal of the site, how many languages do you need, is the content ready, and which features are essential versus nice to have? The clearer the brief, the more accurate the quote, and the lower the risk of the price creeping up halfway through.
